# Magnetic curtains on the sun: The sharpest image of our star’s surface

Photographers and space enthusiasts alike were recently treated to a stunning new image of the sun’s surface, revealing intricate patterns of magnetic fields that resemble curtains dancing in the solar wind. This groundbreaking image, captured by the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope in Hawaii, provides the clearest and most detailed view of the sun’s surface to date. In this blog post, we will explore the significance of this image for photographers and share some practical tips for capturing stunning images of the sun.

## The significance of magnetic curtains on the sun

The sun is a dynamic and ever-changing star, with magnetic fields playing a crucial role in shaping its surface features. The newly captured image of magnetic curtains on the sun’s surface offers unprecedented insights into the complex interplay between magnetic fields and solar activity. These curtains, which are formed by magnetic field lines rising from below the sun’s surface, create mesmerizing patterns that can be seen in high-resolution images like the one captured by the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope.

## Practical tips for photographing the sun

Capturing stunning images of the sun requires careful planning, specialized equipment, and a good understanding of solar photography techniques. Here are some practical tips to help you take your sun photography to the next level:

### 1. Use a solar filter

When photographing the sun, it is crucial to use a solar filter to protect your camera and your eyes from the intense brightness of the sun. Solar filters are specially designed to block out harmful ultraviolet and infrared rays while allowing you to capture detailed images of the sun’s surface.

### 2. Choose the right time of day

The best time to photograph the sun is during sunrise or sunset when the sun is low on the horizon. This not only creates a more visually appealing composition but also reduces the glare and harsh shadows that can obscure details on the sun’s surface.

### 3. Experiment with different camera settings

To capture sharp and detailed images of the sun, experiment with different camera settings such as exposure, ISO, and aperture. A fast shutter speed is essential for capturing the sun’s surface without motion blur, while a low ISO setting can help reduce noise in your images.

### 4. Consider using a telephoto lens

A telephoto lens allows you to zoom in on the sun’s surface and capture intricate details that may not be visible with a standard lens. Look for a lens with a focal length of at least 300mm to get close-up shots of the sun’s surface features.

### 5. Practice patience and persistence

Photographing the sun is a challenging and rewarding endeavor that requires patience and persistence. Keep experimenting with different techniques and settings, and don’t be discouraged if your first attempts are not perfect. With practice and dedication, you can capture stunning images of the sun that rival the sharpest images taken by professional telescopes.
