# The Portfolio Mistake 90% of New Photographers Make

When starting out as a photographer, one of the most important tools you have at your disposal is your portfolio. Your portfolio is essentially your resume in the photography world. It showcases your skills, style, and creativity to potential clients. However, many new photographers make a critical mistake when putting together their portfolio that can hinder their success. In this blog post, we will discuss the portfolio mistake that 90% of new photographers make and provide practical tips on how to avoid it.

![Photographer](/path/to/image)

## The Mistake: Quantity Over Quality

One of the most common mistakes new photographers make when creating their portfolio is focusing on quantity over quality. It can be tempting to include as many photos as possible to showcase the range of work you have done. However, this can actually work against you. Having a portfolio with too many photos can overwhelm potential clients and dilute the impact of your best work.

Instead of trying to include every photo you have ever taken, focus on curating a selection of your best work. Choose images that showcase your skills, creativity, and unique style. Quality is always more important than quantity when it comes to your portfolio.

## Practical Tips for Creating a Strong Portfolio

### 1. Choose Your Best Work

When selecting images for your portfolio, be ruthless in your editing process. Choose only your best work to showcase. Look for images that are technically strong, visually appealing, and represent your unique style. Remember, it's better to have a small selection of stunning images than a large collection of mediocre ones.

### 2. Show Variety

While it's important to showcase your best work, it's also essential to show variety in your portfolio. Include images that demonstrate your range as a photographer. This could include different styles, subjects, and techniques. By showing diversity in your portfolio, you can demonstrate your versatility and attract a wider range of clients.

### 3. Tell a Story

Your portfolio should tell a story about who you are as a photographer. Think about the narrative you want to convey to potential clients. Consider the flow of your images and how they work together to create a cohesive portfolio. By telling a story through your work, you can engage viewers and leave a lasting impression.

### 4. Update Regularly

Your portfolio should be a dynamic and evolving representation of your work. It's essential to update it regularly with your latest and best images. As you grow and develop as a photographer, your portfolio should reflect these changes. By keeping your portfolio fresh and up-to-date, you can show potential clients that you are actively working and improving in your craft.

## Conclusion

Your portfolio is a crucial tool for showcasing your skills and attracting clients as a photographer. By avoiding the common mistake of focusing on quantity over quality, you can create a strong and impactful portfolio that sets you apart from the competition. Remember to choose your best work, show variety, tell a story, and update regularly to create a portfolio that reflects your unique style and talent. By following these practical tips, you can avoid the portfolio mistake that many new photographers make and set yourself up for success in the industry.
