# 5 Frames Looking back at Kodak High Speed Infrared (HIE) through a Nikon FM

In the world of film photography, there are certain films that hold a special place in the hearts of photographers. Kodak High Speed Infrared (HIE) is one such film. Known for its unique infrared properties, HIE was a favorite among photographers looking to create dreamy, otherworldly images. In this blog post, we will take a look back at HIE through the lens of a Nikon FM, exploring its characteristics, practical tips for shooting with it, and the magic it can bring to your images.

## Introduction to Kodak High Speed Infrared (HIE)

Kodak HIE was a high-speed black and white film that was sensitive to infrared light. This sensitivity allowed photographers to capture images with a surreal, ethereal quality, with foliage appearing as bright white and skies appearing dark. HIE was discontinued by Kodak in 2007, much to the dismay of photographers who loved its unique look.

### Characteristics of Kodak HIE

- **Infrared Sensitivity**: HIE was sensitive to infrared light, giving images a surreal, dreamlike quality.
- **High Speed**: With a speed of ISO 400, HIE was a versatile film that could be used in a variety of lighting conditions.
- **Grainy Texture**: HIE had a distinctive grainy texture that added to its unique look.

## Practical Tips for Shooting with Kodak HIE

### 1. Use an Infrared Filter

To enhance the infrared effect of HIE, consider using an infrared filter. This filter will block out visible light, allowing only infrared light to reach the film and creating a more pronounced effect.

### 2. Experiment with Exposure Settings

Because of its unique properties, HIE may require some experimentation with exposure settings. Try shooting a few test frames at different exposures to see what works best for the look you are trying to achieve.

### 3. Embrace the Grain

HIE has a distinctive grainy texture that adds to its unique look. Embrace the grain and use it to your advantage in your compositions.

### 4. Shoot in Bright Sunlight

HIE performs best in bright sunlight, where the infrared effect is most pronounced. Consider shooting on a sunny day to make the most of this film's unique properties.

### 5. Develop with Care

When developing HIE, be sure to work with a lab or developer who is familiar with infrared films. Developing infrared film requires special care to ensure the best results.
