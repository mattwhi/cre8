# Instagram Wasn’t the Problem. Our Obsession Was.

In the world of photography, Instagram has been both a blessing and a curse. On one hand, it has provided a platform for photographers to showcase their work to a wide audience and connect with other like-minded individuals. On the other hand, it has also fueled an unhealthy obsession with likes, followers, and validation. As photographers, it’s important to remember that Instagram wasn’t the problem. Our obsession with it was.

## The Downside of Instagram

Instagram has become a double-edged sword for photographers. While it has allowed us to reach a larger audience and gain recognition for our work, it has also created a culture of comparison and competition. The platform has become a numbers game, with likes and followers determining our worth as photographers. This constant need for validation can lead to feelings of inadequacy and self-doubt. 

![photographer](/path/to/image)

## Practical Tips for Overcoming Instagram Obsession

### 1. Focus on Your Craft

Instead of obsessing over likes and followers, focus on honing your craft and creating meaningful work. Remember why you started taking photos in the first place - because you love it. Take the time to experiment, try new techniques, and push yourself creatively. The more you focus on creating quality work, the less you’ll care about the numbers on Instagram.

### 2. Limit Your Time on the Platform

It’s easy to get sucked into the black hole of Instagram scrolling. Set limits for yourself on how much time you spend on the platform each day. Schedule specific times for posting and engaging with others, and then log off. Remember, there’s a whole world outside of Instagram waiting to be explored.

### 3. Connect with Other Photographers

Instead of viewing other photographers as competition, see them as potential collaborators and friends. Reach out to fellow photographers whose work you admire and start a conversation. Share tips, feedback, and encouragement with each other. Building a supportive community of like-minded individuals can help combat feelings of isolation and competition.

### 4. Practice Gratitude

Instead of focusing on what you don’t have (likes, followers, recognition), practice gratitude for what you do have. Take a moment each day to reflect on the things you’re grateful for in your photography journey - whether it’s a beautiful sunset you captured, a new lens you purchased, or a kind comment from a follower. Gratitude can shift your perspective from scarcity to abundance.

## In Conclusion

Instagram wasn’t the problem. Our obsession with it was. As photographers, it’s important to remember that our worth isn’t determined by the number of likes or followers we have. Focus on your craft, limit your time on the platform, connect with other photographers, and practice gratitude. By shifting your mindset and priorities, you can overcome Instagram obsession and rediscover the joy of photography.
