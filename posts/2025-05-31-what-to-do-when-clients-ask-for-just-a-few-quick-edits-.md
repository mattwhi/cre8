# What to Do When Clients Ask for 'Just a Few Quick Edits'

As a photographer, you know that editing is a crucial part of the post-production process. It’s where you bring your vision to life and make your photos truly stand out. However, there are times when clients may ask for “just a few quick edits” that can end up taking more time than expected. In this blog post, we’ll discuss some practical tips on how to handle these requests effectively and efficiently.

## Introduction

When a client asks for “just a few quick edits,” it’s important to understand their expectations and communicate clearly about what is feasible within the scope of the project. While it’s understandable that clients may want to see some changes to the photos, it’s also important to set boundaries and manage their expectations. In this blog post, we’ll cover some strategies on how to navigate these requests without compromising the quality of your work or spending too much time on revisions.

## Practical Tips

1. **Set Clear Expectations**: From the very beginning, make sure to communicate with your clients about the editing process and what is included in your services. Let them know how many rounds of revisions are included and what type of edits can be done within that scope. By setting clear boundaries, you can avoid endless back-and-forth requests for additional changes.

2. **Create a Style Guide**: To streamline the editing process and ensure consistency in your work, consider creating a style guide that outlines your editing preferences. This can include information on color correction, skin retouching, cropping, and any other specific techniques you use. By sharing this guide with your clients, you can help them understand your editing style and minimize the need for extensive revisions.

3. **Use Editing Software Wisely**: When clients ask for “just a few quick edits,” it can be tempting to make the changes directly in Photoshop or Lightroom. However, this can lead to a time-consuming process of going back and forth with the client on each individual edit. Instead, consider using editing software like Capture One or Luminar that allows for non-destructive editing and easy batch processing. This can help you make quick changes without compromising the quality of your work.

4. **Offer Limited Revisions**: To avoid endless revisions and ensure that the editing process stays on track, consider offering a limited number of revisions as part of your package. This can help you manage your time effectively and prevent clients from making excessive requests for changes. Be clear about the number of revisions included in your services and set a timeline for when they can be requested.

5. **Educate Your Clients**: Sometimes, clients may not fully understand the editing process and the time it takes to make changes to photos. Take the time to educate your clients about the editing workflow and the effort that goes into each edit. By setting realistic expectations and explaining the process, you can help clients understand the value of your work and the importance of respecting your time.

## Conclusion

When clients ask for “just a few quick edits,” it’s important to handle these requests with professionalism and efficiency. By setting clear expectations, creating a style guide, using editing software wisely, offering limited revisions, and educating your clients, you can navigate these requests effectively and ensure that the editing process runs smoothly. Remember that your time and expertise are valuable, so don’t be afraid to set boundaries and communicate openly with your clients about what is feasible within the scope of the project. With these practical tips, you can deliver high-quality edits that exceed your clients’ expectations while staying on schedule and within budget.
