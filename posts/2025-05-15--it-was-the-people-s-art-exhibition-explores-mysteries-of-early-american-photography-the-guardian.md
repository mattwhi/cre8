# ‘It was the people’s art’: exhibition explores mysteries of early American photography - The Guardian

The early days of photography in America were a time of innovation and experimentation. A new exhibition at the Museum of Modern Art in New York City is shining a light on this fascinating period in the history of photography. Titled ‘It was the people’s art’, the exhibition explores the mysteries of early American photography and showcases the work of some of the country’s earliest photographers.

![Early American Photography](/path/to/image)

## Uncovering the Secrets of Early American Photography

The exhibition features a diverse range of photographs, from daguerreotypes to ambrotypes, highlighting the different techniques and styles that were popular during the 19th century. Visitors to the exhibition will have the opportunity to see rare and never-before-seen photographs that offer a glimpse into the lives of early Americans.

One of the highlights of the exhibition is a collection of portraits taken by pioneering photographer Mathew Brady. Brady is best known for his portraits of prominent figures such as Abraham Lincoln and Ulysses S. Grant, but the exhibition also showcases his lesser-known work, including portraits of everyday Americans.

## Practical Tips for Photographers

As a photographer, exploring the history of early American photography can provide valuable insights and inspiration for your own work. Here are some practical tips for photographers looking to incorporate elements of early American photography into their practice:

- Experiment with different photographic techniques, such as tintypes and cyanotypes, to create unique and visually striking images.
- Study the work of early American photographers, such as Mathew Brady and Alexander Gardner, to gain a better understanding of the history and evolution of photography.
- Embrace the imperfections and limitations of early photographic processes, such as long exposure times and unpredictable results, to add a sense of authenticity and nostalgia to your work.

By incorporating elements of early American photography into your practice, you can create images that are not only visually compelling but also rich in history and meaning.

## Conclusion

The ‘It was the people’s art’ exhibition at the Museum of Modern Art offers a rare opportunity to explore the mysteries of early American photography and gain a deeper appreciation for the art form. By studying the work of early American photographers and experimenting with different techniques, photographers can incorporate elements of this fascinating period in the history of photography into their own practice.

Whether you are a seasoned professional or an amateur enthusiast, the history of early American photography has much to offer in terms of inspiration and creativity. Take the time to explore the exhibition and immerse yourself in the world of early American photography – you never know what secrets you might uncover.
