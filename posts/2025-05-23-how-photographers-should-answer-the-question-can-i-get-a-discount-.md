# How Photographers Should Answer the Question “Can I Get A Discount?”

As a photographer, you've likely encountered the question "Can I get a discount?" from potential clients at some point in your career. While it can be tempting to offer a discount to secure a booking, it's important to consider how it may impact your business in the long run. In this blog post, we'll discuss how photographers should handle this question and provide some practical tips on how to respond effectively.

## Introduction

One of the most challenging aspects of running a photography business is pricing your services. You want to attract clients and stay competitive in the market, but you also need to make a profit to sustain your business. When a client asks for a discount, it can put you in a tough spot. On one hand, you want to accommodate their request and secure the booking, but on the other hand, you don't want to devalue your work or set a precedent for future clients.

## Practical Tips for Responding to Discount Requests

### 1. Educate Your Clients

One of the best ways to handle discount requests is to educate your clients on the value of your services. Explain the time, effort, and expertise that goes into capturing and editing photos. Help them understand why your prices are what they are and how they compare to other photographers in the area. By highlighting the quality of your work and the experience you provide, you can justify your pricing and discourage clients from seeking discounts.

### 2. Offer Value-Added Services

Instead of discounting your prices, consider offering value-added services to sweeten the deal for your clients. This could include complimentary prints, extra editing time, or a free engagement session. By adding value to your packages, you can show clients that they are getting more for their money without compromising your pricing.

### 3. Stick to Your Policies

It's important to have clear policies in place regarding discounts and pricing. If a client asks for a discount, refer them to your pricing guide or contract that outlines your rates and terms. By sticking to your policies, you establish yourself as a professional and avoid setting a precedent for future clients to ask for discounts.

### 4. Be Transparent

If you do decide to offer a discount, be transparent with your client about why you are doing so. Maybe it's a slow season and you have availability to fill, or perhaps the client is booking multiple sessions with you. Whatever the reason, communicate openly with your client so they understand the rationale behind the discount.

### 5. Know When to Say No

While it can be tempting to offer a discount to secure a booking, it's important to know when to say no. If a client is consistently asking for discounts or trying to negotiate your prices, they may not be the right fit for your business. It's better to walk away from a potential client who doesn't value your work than to compromise your pricing and devalue your services.

## Conclusion

As a photographer, it's important to value your work and price your services accordingly. While it can be challenging to navigate discount requests from clients, it's essential to stand firm in your pricing and policies. By educating your clients, offering value-added services, and being transparent in your communication, you can effectively handle discount requests while maintaining the integrity of your business. Remember, it's okay to say no to a discount if it doesn't align with your business goals and values. Your clients will respect you more for it in the long run.
