# Historic Vietnam War photo has 'suspended attribution' after doubts of photographers ownership - New York Post

In the world of photography, attribution is everything. It is what gives credit to the person behind the lens, the artist who captured that moment in time. However, what happens when the ownership of a photograph is called into question? This is exactly the case with a historic Vietnam War photo that has recently had its attribution suspended after doubts arose about the photographer's ownership.

The iconic image in question depicts a group of soldiers trudging through a rice paddy during the Vietnam War. The photo has been widely circulated and used in various publications over the years, but its true origins have come under scrutiny. According to a recent report by the *New York Post*, doubts have been raised about who actually took the photograph, leading to the suspension of its attribution.

## The importance of proper attribution

Proper attribution is not just a formality in the world of photography; it is a matter of respect and recognition for the artist behind the lens. When a photographer's work is used without proper credit, it not only diminishes their contribution to the art form but also undermines the integrity of the entire industry.

In the case of the Vietnam War photo, the photographer's identity is crucial not only for recognition but also for historical accuracy. Knowing the person behind the lens can provide valuable context and insight into the circumstances surrounding the image's capture.

## Practical tips for photographers

As photographers, it is essential to protect your work and ensure proper attribution at all times. Here are some practical tips to help you safeguard your photographs and maintain ownership:

1. **Watermark your images**: Adding a visible watermark to your photos can deter unauthorized use and help identify your work.
2. **Register your copyright**: Consider registering your images with the U.S. Copyright Office to establish a legal record of ownership.
3. **Monitor usage**: Keep an eye out for any unauthorized use of your photos online or in print. Utilize reverse image search tools to track where your images are being used.
4. **Include metadata**: Embed metadata in your digital files to include information about the photographer, copyright, and licensing terms.

By taking these proactive steps, you can help protect your work and ensure that you receive proper credit for your photographs.
