# Another Yellowstone “Touron” Gored by Bison After Getting Too Close

Yellowstone National Park is a photographer's paradise, with its stunning landscapes and abundant wildlife. However, it can also be a dangerous place if visitors do not respect the animals and their space. Unfortunately, another "touron" (tourist + moron) has been gored by a bison after getting too close for the perfect shot.

## Introduction

The incident occurred near the Fairy Falls trail in the park, where a group of tourists had gathered to watch a herd of bison grazing. One tourist, eager to get a closer look, approached the herd and attempted to take a selfie with one of the animals. The bison, feeling threatened, charged at the tourist and gored him in the leg before fleeing the scene.

This is not an isolated incident. Every year, numerous tourists are injured by wildlife in Yellowstone National Park due to their reckless behavior. It is important for photographers to remember that these animals are wild and unpredictable, and should be treated with respect and caution at all times.

## Practical Tips for Photographing Wildlife in Yellowstone

1. **Keep your distance**: The National Park Service recommends staying at least 25 yards away from bison and other large animals, and 100 yards away from predators such as wolves and bears. Use a telephoto lens to capture close-up shots without getting too close.

2. **Observe from a safe distance**: Instead of approaching the animals, observe them from a safe distance and let them go about their natural behaviors. This will result in more authentic and captivating photographs.

3. **Be aware of your surroundings**: Always be mindful of your surroundings and the behavior of the animals. Look for signs of agitation, such as raised tails or ears, and be prepared to move away quickly if necessary.

4. **Respect the animals**: Remember that you are a guest in the animals' home, and it is important to respect their space and behavior. Do not attempt to feed, touch, or approach them for the sake of a photo.

## Conclusion

Photographing wildlife in Yellowstone National Park can be a rewarding experience, but it is essential to prioritize safety and respect for the animals. By following these practical tips and maintaining a safe distance, photographers can capture stunning images without putting themselves or the animals at risk. Remember, the safety and well-being of the wildlife should always come first. Let's strive to be responsible photographers and stewards of the environment, ensuring that these magnificent creatures are protected for future generations to enjoy.
