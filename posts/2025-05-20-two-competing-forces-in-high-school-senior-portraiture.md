# Two Competing Forces in High School Senior Portraiture

As a photographer specializing in high school senior portraits, you are faced with two competing forces that can greatly impact the success of your work. On one hand, you have the desire to capture the unique personality and essence of each senior in a creative and artistic way. On the other hand, you have to meet the expectations of the senior and their parents, who often have specific ideas and preferences for how they want the portraits to look.

In this blog post, we will explore these two competing forces and provide practical tips for navigating them to create stunning senior portraits that satisfy both the artistic vision and the expectations of your clients.

## Introduction

High school senior portraits are an important milestone for many young adults as they transition from adolescence to adulthood. These portraits serve as a lasting memory of this significant time in their lives and are often displayed proudly in yearbooks, on social media, and in family homes.

As a photographer, you have the opportunity to capture the unique personality, style, and essence of each senior in a way that reflects who they are and how they want to be remembered. However, this creative vision can sometimes clash with the expectations and desires of the senior and their parents, who may have specific ideas about how they want the portraits to look.

## The Creative Force

![Senior Portrait](/path/to/seniorportrait.jpg)

The creative force in high school senior portraiture is driven by your artistic vision and desire to capture the essence of each senior in a unique and meaningful way. This force pushes you to experiment with different poses, lighting techniques, and settings to create portraits that are both visually stunning and emotionally resonant.

To harness the creative force in your senior portraits, consider the following tips:

- Get to know your senior: Take the time to talk to the senior and learn about their interests, hobbies, and personality. This will help you tailor the portrait session to reflect who they are and what makes them unique.

- Experiment with different poses and angles: Don't be afraid to try out unconventional poses and angles to create dynamic and visually interesting portraits.

- Use natural light: Whenever possible, use natural light to create soft, flattering portraits that highlight the senior's features and personality.

## The Expectation Force

While the creative force drives you to push the boundaries of traditional senior portraiture, the expectation force pulls you back to meet the desires and expectations of your clients. Seniors and their parents often have specific ideas about how they want the portraits to look, which can sometimes limit your creative freedom.

To navigate the expectation force in your senior portraits, consider the following tips:

- Have a pre-session consultation: Before the portrait session, sit down with the senior and their parents to discuss their vision for the portraits. This will help you understand their expectations and tailor the session to meet their needs.

- Show examples: Bring examples of your previous work to the consultation to give the senior and their parents a sense of your style and approach to senior portraits. This can help manage their expectations and ensure they are happy with the final results.

- Be open to feedback: Listen to the feedback and suggestions of the senior and their parents during the portrait session. While it's important to stay true to your artistic vision, being open to feedback can help you create portraits that satisfy your clients.

## Conclusion

In high school senior portraiture, photographers are faced with two competing forces: the creative force that drives them to push the boundaries of traditional portraiture and capture the unique essence of each senior, and the expectation force that pulls them back to meet the desires and expectations of their clients.

By harnessing the creative force through experimentation, getting to know the senior, and using natural light, and navigating the expectation force through pre-session consultations, showing examples, and being open to feedback, photographers can create stunning senior portraits that satisfy both their artistic vision and their clients' expectations.

Balancing these two competing forces is no easy task, but with practice, patience, and a willingness to collaborate with clients, photographers can create senior portraits that are both visually stunning and emotionally resonant, capturing the essence of this important milestone in a young adult's life.
