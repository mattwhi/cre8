## Introduction to Kodak HIE

Kodak High Speed Infrared (HIE) was a black and white infrared film that was discontinued in 2007. This film had a unique sensitivity to infrared light, allowing it to capture images with a surreal and otherworldly quality. The film had a distinctive grain structure and a tendency to produce deep blacks and bright whites, making it ideal for creating high-contrast images with a dramatic flair.

### Key Characteristics of Kodak HIE:
- Unique sensitivity to infrared light

## Shooting with Kodak HIE and a Nikon FM

When shooting with Kodak HIE and a Nikon FM, there are a few things to keep in mind to get the best results. Here are some practical tips for making the most of this iconic combination:

1. **Use a red or infrared filter:** To enhance the infrared effect of Kodak HIE, consider using a red or infrared filter when shooting. This will help to block out visible light and allow the film to capture more infrared wavelengths.

2. **Shoot in bright sunlight:** Kodak HIE performs best in bright sunlight, as this is when the infrared light is most intense. Avoid shooting in overcast conditions or in the shade, as this can result in flat and uninteresting images.

3. **Experiment with exposure:** Due to its unique sensitivity to infrared light, Kodak HIE can produce unpredictable results when it comes to exposure. Be prepared to experiment with different exposure settings to find the right balance between capturing detail and maintaining the film's signature high contrast.

4. **Embrace the grain:** Kodak HIE has a distinctive grain structure that adds to its unique aesthetic. Embrace the grain and use it to your advantage, creating images that have a gritty and textural quality.

5. **Consider post-processing:** Kodak HIE can benefit from some post-processing to enhance its unique characteristics. Experiment with adjusting contrast, brightness, and tonality to bring out the best in your images.
